# By fiat

*Notes on how projected entities come into being and continue being:
the solver as the first namer of lakes, granularity as an accounting
policy, and identity sustained by registers.*

Companion to `near-and-far.md`, which distinguishes resident from
projected populations, and to `ontology.md`, whose fiat objects these
are. The question here is the one Smith keeps returning to across the
boundary papers and the document-act papers: fiat entities are not
found, they are *made* — by naming, marking, registering, dwelling —
and they persist only as long as the relating persists. What follows
is the observation that in this game the first agent to perform such
acts is not the player. It is the erosion solver.

## The solver names the lakes

The lake census is not cartography for a vacation. The stream-power
solver cannot route water across a depression cell by cell — the
depression has no downhill until it is understood as a unit that
fills to a spill. So `census_lakes` takes π₀ of the flooded region
and the solver thereafter relates to *lake 12*: one spill, one base
level, one hydraulic unit. Water in the basin is not tracked cubic
metre by cubic metre; it is posted to the lake's account.

This is a fiat act in exactly Smith's sense, performed for exactly
the reason he gives: the economy of relating. An agent that needed
to track every part would drown in its own domain; drawing a fiat
boundary and issuing an identifier is what makes the relation
affordable. The ontology essay's rule — *simulate in fields,
experience in objects* — understates the case: the simulation also
experiences in objects, precisely wherever field-granularity would
bankrupt it. Coarse-graining is fiat-object formation. Physics has
always worked this way; a droplet, a vortex, and a phase are the
fiat objects of agents who could not afford molecules.

## Granularity is an accounting policy

Put the census beside the ledger arc and the two vocabularies fuse.
A cell of a partition is an account: to say "it is in lake 12" is to
post to an aggregate instead of enumerating its parts, which is what
every ledger does — no accountant tracks individual coins. The chart
of accounts is a system of fiat boundaries drawn over the flow of
value for the economy of whoever must relate to it; the lake census
is a chart of accounts drawn over the flow of water. Choosing a
granularity — for the solver, for the renderer, for the books — is
choosing which distinctions you will pay to maintain, and fiat
objects are the accounts that choice opens.

The lakes are moreover *buffers* in the strict sense the runtime
essays use: bounded holdings between cause and effect, with capacity
(the spill elevation), occupancy (the water surface), and overflow
routing (the spill receiver). A lake is a warehouse in the hydrology
pipeline, and the census is the ledger that lets the solver bank
with it.

## Identity is sustained by a register

Smith's answer to how fiat entities *continue* being is documentary:
deeds, cadastres, credentials, registers — document acts, his
extension of speech acts to the paper (and now the schema) that
outlives the utterance. A parcel persists because the cadastre does;
the name binds because the register is kept.

This is the missing half of the genidentity problem from
`near-and-far.md`. A projected population re-derived from the field
each step has no identity *unless someone keeps the register*:
yesterday's lake 12 and today's are connected only by an act of
tracking, recorded — overlap matched, merges and splits posted as
events. The generational handle of a population domain is a document
act; the registry of lakes across geological steps would be the
game's cadastre, and keeping it is not overhead on the simulation
but the very mechanism by which the world has persistent things
rather than momentary analyses.

The trail system has practiced all of this from the beginning,
without the vocabulary. Walkers wear marks into the ground; the
marks steer walkers; the home base is a place made special by
dwelling — intentionality inscribed in the world, a record that is
also a cause, fitting world-to-map and map-to-world at once as
`ontology.md` says a cadastre does. Trails are the game's first
documents, written in ground. When the player someday names a
mountain or claims a shore, they will not be introducing fiat acts
into the world; they will be joining a registry the solver opened
and the walkers have been countersigning all along. The landmark
namer — stream, confluence, mouth, waterfall, lake — is the
solver's register being read back as a gazetteer.

## Shared boundaries, and why seams can close

One technical gift from *Drawing Boundaries*: bona fide boundaries
cannot genuinely coincide — physical contact is molecular proximity,
belongingness only statistical — but fiat boundaries can be
*identically shared*: two parcels, two counties, hold one boundary
in common. The watershed divide between two catchments is such a
shared fiat boundary; so is the seam between two render tiles. That
is why the gluing condition of `near-and-far.md` is satisfiable at
all: the seam is not two edges approximating each other but one
fiat edge owned by both sides, and crack-free rendering is the
discipline of keeping it single. Where the fiat picture holds,
exact agreement is available; physical objects never get that.

## Guardrails

As with its companions, nothing here obliges work. It sets the
interpretation for when the work comes: the lake registry, when
water bodies get their population domain, should be understood as a
cadastre — kept across steps, posting merges and splits, the
authority for identity rather than a cache of analysis. And the
eventual player-facing acts — naming, claiming, home-making — should
be designed as entries in the same register, because that is what
they are. The world's persistent things are exactly as persistent as
its books.
